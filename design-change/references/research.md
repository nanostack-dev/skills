# Evidence ledger: ADR and software design changes

Reviewed on 2026-10-12. Corpus: **20 substantive primary-source pages/documents read**, across 11 source owners (Cognitect/Nygard, AWS, Rust, Kubernetes, C4/Simon Brown, arc42, Mermaid, Cucumber, Stripe, W3C, Lamport/Microsoft Research). Four C4 pages and three arc42 pages deliberately cover different views rather than inflate a count with duplicate advice. Each takeaway below is paraphrased; implications and the final recommendations are our synthesis, not statements of a universal standard. Selected sections of the 34-page PlusCal paper were read, not the entire paper.

## Synthesis for the skill (our recommendations)

1. **Two complementary outputs.** A design-change document teaches the full change, while one or more linked ADRs preserve individual decisions and why they were chosen [1–4]. Do not blur proposed design, accepted decision, implementation, and verification status.
2. **A consistent reading order.** Problem and measurable outcomes → big picture → existing baseline → entity/relationship delta → service and provider flow → pseudocode/invariants → options/decision → acceptance scenarios → migration/rollback → open questions. The exact reading order is our synthesis, informed by the separation of views and user-facing examples [3, 5–11].
3. **Before / After / Changes entity modes.** Keep stable entity IDs, names, positions, scale, and unchanged anchors when feasible. Before is complete enough to understand the old relationships; After is a coherent proposed model; Changes is an overlay or table, not a replacement for either. Each changed relationship explains old and new cardinality. Show removed entities/edges in Before and the change list. Fixed layouts and toggles are our memory-aid design, not a sourced C4/ER mandate [8, 12, 19].
4. **One semantic legend.** Use text badges and optional color for UNCHANGED, ADDED, CHANGED, REMOVED. Do not hijack domain notation (ER identifying lines, async arrows) as change markers. Separate evidence status (Observed / Proposed / Unknown) from change status. A proposed element can be ADDED but is never thereby Observed [8, 12, 13, 19].
5. **Three questions, three views.** Context answers who depends on us and our boundary; entity model answers what concepts and relationships change; sequence answers how a chosen request/event crosses services and third parties. Do not compress all three into a single graph [5–13].
6. **A consistent worked example.** Carry one realistic user operation through entities, sequence steps, pseudocode, and acceptance scenarios. Assign small IDs (FLOW-1 / INV-1 / AC-1 / DEC-1) for traceability. This cross-view convention is our synthesis; the sources motivate concrete examples and consistent mappings [3, 9–11, 14].
7. **Third-party contract card.** Record ownership/source of truth, authentication/trust boundary, transmitted data, synchronous calls versus callbacks, timeout/retry owner, idempotency identity/window, event ordering, duplicate handling, and recovery/reconciliation where relevant. Link the exact provider docs and version; unknown values remain unknown [10, 15–17].
8. **Failure semantics must be visible.** Where relevant, include provider timeout after a possible commit, duplicate or out-of-order callback, permanent refusal, partial local/external failure, and retry exhaustion. Pseudocode and acceptance scenarios must agree on observable Pending/Failed/Completed or actual domain states. Do not promise an external effect is absent merely because a request timed out [15–17, 20].
9. **Acceptance is a proposed contract.** Keep Given/When/Then in domain language. Include happy path plus the material failure/authorization/migration conditions relevant to the change. Distinguish acceptance scenarios, a test implementation plan, and actual execution evidence. Numeric criteria come from owner requirements, measurements, or labeled proposals [4, 11, 14].
10. **Migration is a real state.** Document Existing → compatibility preparation → optional backfill/reconciliation → activation → observation → retirement. Name rollback targets, checkpoint and signals, including what cannot be reversed. Avoid a simplistic Before/After-only account for data or protocol changes [4, 18].
11. **Readable visual artifact plus durable prose.** Interactive views can help compare modes, explore failure branches, and highlight a selected entity across views. Also supply Markdown with diagram sources, a change table, short flow narratives, and decision records so meaning survives screenshot/export or renderer failure. Interactive toggles and cross-highlighting are our synthesis; only general readability/notation and color cues are directly supported by [8, 19].
12. **Scale by change risk.** Small ADR-only requests stay compact; cross-service/data/provider/migration changes get the relevant views. Mark a genuinely inapplicable view briefly rather than inventing services, entities, pseudocode, third parties, or tests to satisfy the template [1, 7, 9, 10].

## Concrete review questions for generated designs

- Can a newcomer explain the existing system before reading the proposed change? Are baseline facts anchored to code/current docs rather than inferred from a desired design?
- Are all added/changed/removed entities and relationships traceable between Before, After, and the change table? Are cardinalities and semantic line styles preserved?
- Are the same domain names and invariants used in every view? Does each flow participant exist in the context/container view?
- Can a reviewer locate the local commit, external call, async delivery, unknown-outcome point, and duplicate/retry policy in the selected flow?
- Does every important decision have alternatives including keeping the status quo, tradeoffs, constraints, and a reason grounded in the project?
- Are acceptance outcomes observable, measurable when required, and clearly separate from claims that tests have passed?
- Can old and new versions coexist during migration? What is the last safe rollback point and what data/external effects survive rollback?
- Are proposed assumptions and unresolved owner/provider facts visible, with evidence status separate from change badges?
